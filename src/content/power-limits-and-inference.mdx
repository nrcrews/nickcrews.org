An H100 will draw up to 700 watts. One command, `nvidia-smi -pl`, tells it to draw less. I wanted
to know what that costs an inference server, and the answer turned out to depend on which half of
inference you ask.

## Two jobs, one chip

Serving a model is two different workloads sharing a GPU.

Prefill reads the prompt. The model's weights are pulled from memory once and applied to thousands
of prompt tokens at a time, so the arithmetic dominates and the cores are the bottleneck.

Decode writes the answer, one token per request per step. Each step pulls every weight from memory
again but does only a sliver of math with it. Here memory is the bottleneck. The cores finish
early and wait.

A power cap works through a single lever: it lowers the core clock. That should hurt the phase
that needs the cores and spare the one that's waiting on memory. I measured how much.

I ran vLLM on eight H100 SXM cards, four serving Qwen3-8B and four serving Qwen3-14B, and stepped
each card from 700 W down to 200 W with 32 requests in flight. Each card is compared against its
own uncapped run. The two models land within a few points of each other, so the charts show the
8B. Code and raw data are on [GitHub](https://github.com/nrcrews/tokens-per-watt).

## Decode barely notices

At 415 W, about 60% of the card's maximum, decode keeps 96% of its speed. Prefill keeps 74%.

![Speed against power limit](/images/power-limits-and-inference/speed.png)

*The shaded region is where decode keeps at least 95% of its uncapped speed.*

The clock shows why. At 415 W both phases run about a quarter fewer cycles per second. Prefill
loses that same quarter of its throughput, because every cycle was doing useful math. Decode loses
4%, because most of its cycles were spent waiting for memory, and memory doesn't slow down when the
core clock does.

![Core clock against power limit](/images/power-limits-and-inference/clock.png)

*Decode holds its full clock down to 555 W. Uncapped, it draws only about 525 W, so the top of
the power range does nothing for it.*

## Slower is cheaper

Running slower also makes each token cheaper. A chip's dynamic power scales with frequency times
voltage squared, and a lower clock lets the voltage come down with it. Energy falls faster than
speed.

At 415 W, decode produces 23% more tokens per joule and prefill 25%. No other setting gives both
phases a gain that large.

![Tokens per joule against power limit](/images/power-limits-and-inference/efficiency.png)

*Decode's efficiency climbs until about 345 W and then levels off, but by then it's 8% slower.*

## Then it falls off a cliff

Below about 345 W the clock collapses. At 200 W, decode runs at 345 MHz, a sixth of its uncapped
clock, and keeps only about half its speed. Prefill gets there first: between 415 W and 345 W its
clock drops by a third while the limit drops by only a sixth.

My best guess is the voltage floor. Above it, the chip saves power by lowering both voltage and
clock, which is cheap. Once voltage can't go any lower, every watt has to come out of the clock
alone, while memory and the rest of the board keep drawing their share. I didn't measure voltage,
so treat that as a hypothesis.

Batch size moves the cliff, too. More requests in flight means more math per weight read, and
decode starts to look like prefill.

![Decode speed by requests in flight](/images/power-limits-and-inference/concurrency.png)

*At 270 W, decode keeps 85% of its speed with one request in flight and 71% with 32.*

The 350 W H100 PCIe tells the same story without the cliff. At 245 W, decode keeps 96% of its
speed for 21% more tokens per joule. At 200 W it's still at 87%, with the clock near 1 GHz.

## What it means

Decode doesn't need a full-power GPU. On an H100 SXM, 415 W costs it 4% of its speed and saves
about a fifth of the energy per token. Prefill does need the power: it slows in step with the
clock.

So the right power limit depends on the job. A GPU doing mostly decode can be capped hard. One
doing mostly prefill shouldn't be. Neither should go below the cliff.

These numbers count the GPU alone, not the CPU, network or cooling around it, and cover two models
on one engine. Next I want to measure voltage and memory clock through the cliff, to see whether
the floor is really what's breaking. Everything needed to rerun this on your own GPU is in the
[repo](https://github.com/nrcrews/tokens-per-watt).
